# Fixed braces

## What it is
Fixed braces are brackets bonded to the teeth and connected by an archwire that is adjusted over time. Brackets may be metal, tooth-coloured ceramic, or lingual (behind the teeth).

## Who it suits
All degrees of crowding, spacing, rotations and bite problems, including complex cases that aligners handle poorly. They don't depend on the patient remembering to wear trays.

## How it works
Brackets are bonded in one visit. Adjustments every 4–8 weeks gradually move teeth. Elastics may be used to correct the bite.

## Timeline and results
Usually 12–24 months. Retainers are needed afterwards to hold the result.

## Risks and side effects
Soreness after adjustments, mouth ulcers from brackets, and a higher risk of decay and white spots if brushing around brackets is poor. Some foods (hard, sticky) must be avoided.

## Cost factors
Metal braces are generally the most affordable option. Ceramic and lingual systems cost more. Case complexity and duration matter most.
