# Why teeth change colour

## Extrinsic (surface) stains
Coffee, tea, red wine, cola, curry and turmeric, tobacco, and some mouthwashes (chlorhexidine) stain the enamel surface. Professional cleaning removes much of this, and whitening handles the rest.

## Intrinsic discolouration
- **Ageing:** enamel thins and the yellower dentine underneath shows through.
- **Trauma:** a knocked tooth may darken (grey or brown) if the nerve is damaged, which should be checked by a dentist.
- **Medication and fluoride:** some antibiotics (tetracyclines) taken in childhood, or excess fluoride (fluorosis), cause bands or spots.
- **Decay and old fillings:** brown or black spots can be decay, and grey areas can show through from old amalgam fillings.

## What that means for treatment
General yellowing responds well to whitening. Grey, banded or single dark teeth respond less predictably; bonding, veneers or treating the underlying cause (e.g. root canal treatment for a dead tooth) may be better options. Dark spots should be examined for decay before any cosmetic treatment.
