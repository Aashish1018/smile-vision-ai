# Porcelain veneers

## What it is
Veneers are thin shells of porcelain (ceramic) bonded to the front of teeth to change their colour, shape or length. They are stain-resistant and very natural-looking.

## Who it suits
People with discolouration that whitening cannot fix, worn or chipped front teeth, or mildly uneven teeth who prefer not to have orthodontics. Veneers do not fix bite problems or significant crowding; aligners or braces may be recommended first. Heavy grinding is a relative contraindication.

## How it works
Usually two or three visits: planning (often with a digital or wax mock-up), preparation where a thin layer of enamel (around 0.3–0.7 mm) is removed and impressions or scans taken, and fitting. Temporary veneers may be worn in between.

## Timeline and results
Porcelain veneers commonly last 10–15 years with good care. Because enamel is removed, the treatment is not reversible: a veneer will always need replacing with another restoration.

## Risks and side effects
Short-term sensitivity, the possibility of chipping or debonding, and the irreversibility of enamel removal. A night guard may be advised for grinders.

## Cost factors
Priced per tooth. Ceramic type, lab work and the number of teeth (often 4–10 for a smile makeover) drive the total.
