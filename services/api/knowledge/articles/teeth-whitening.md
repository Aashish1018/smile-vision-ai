# Teeth whitening

## What it is
Whitening lightens the natural colour of tooth enamel using a peroxide-based gel (hydrogen peroxide or carbamide peroxide). It works on *intrinsic* yellowing and on *extrinsic* stains from coffee, tea, red wine and tobacco. It does not change the colour of crowns, veneers, fillings or bonding.

## Who it suits
Good candidates have healthy teeth and gums with general yellowing or surface staining. Whitening is usually postponed if there is untreated decay, gum disease, exposed roots or very worn enamel; these are treated first. Grey or blue-grey discolouration (for example from tetracycline or a dead nerve) responds less predictably, and bonding or veneers may be discussed instead.

## How it works
- **In-office whitening:** a dentist protects the gums and applies a higher-strength gel, sometimes with a light, for 45–90 minutes. Visible change in one visit.
- **Dentist-supervised home whitening:** custom trays with a lower-strength gel, worn daily for 2–4 weeks.
- Over-the-counter strips and toothpastes are weaker; whitening toothpaste mainly removes surface stain.

## Timeline and results
Results typically last 6 months to 3 years depending on diet and smoking. Touch-up sessions maintain the shade.

## Risks and side effects
Temporary tooth sensitivity and gum irritation are the most common. Gels used by non-dental providers may be unregulated or too strong. Existing restorations will not lighten and may need replacing to match.

## Cost factors
In-office whitening costs more than supervised home kits. Pre-treatment cleaning is often recommended and priced separately.
