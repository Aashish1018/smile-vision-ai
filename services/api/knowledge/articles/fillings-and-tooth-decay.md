# Tooth decay and fillings

## What it is
Tooth decay (caries) happens when bacteria in plaque turn sugars into acids that dissolve enamel. Early decay may look like chalky white spots; later it shows as brown or black spots or holes. Decay is confirmed by a dentist with an examination and often X-rays. A photo can only suggest it.

## Who it suits
Fillings repair cavities once decay has broken through enamel. Very early white-spot lesions can sometimes be reversed with fluoride and better diet and hygiene.

## How it works
The decayed part is removed, usually under local anaesthetic, and the space filled with tooth-coloured composite, glass ionomer or, less commonly now, amalgam. Most fillings take one visit.

## Timeline and results
Composite fillings commonly last 5–10 years.

## Risks and side effects
Short-term sensitivity. Deep decay may reach the nerve and need root canal treatment. Untreated decay can cause pain, abscesses and tooth loss, which is why it should be treated **before** cosmetic work like whitening or veneers.

## Cost factors
Size and number of surfaces, material, and the number of teeth.
