# Root canal treatment

## What it is
Root canal treatment removes infected or inflamed pulp (the nerve and blood supply) from inside a tooth, cleans and disinfects the canals, and seals them. It saves a tooth that would otherwise need extraction.

## Who it suits
Teeth with deep decay, cracks or trauma that have caused pulp inflammation or infection. Symptoms can include lingering sensitivity to hot or cold, pain on biting, swelling, or a darkened tooth, but sometimes there are no symptoms at all.

## How it works
One to three visits under local anaesthetic. Back teeth usually need a crown afterwards to protect them from fracture.

## Timeline and results
Success rates are high, and a treated tooth can last a lifetime with a good restoration.

## Risks and side effects
Tenderness for a few days, occasional need for retreatment, and a risk of fracture if the tooth isn't crowned.

## Cost factors
Front teeth (one canal) cost less than molars (three or four canals). A crown is usually an additional cost.
