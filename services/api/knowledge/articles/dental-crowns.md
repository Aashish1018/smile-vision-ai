# Dental crowns

## What it is
A crown is a cap that covers the whole visible part of a tooth to restore its strength, shape and appearance. Common materials are zirconia, lithium disilicate (e-max), porcelain fused to metal, and gold.

## Who it suits
Teeth that are heavily filled, cracked, badly broken or worn, teeth after root canal treatment (especially back teeth), and as the visible part of a dental implant. For purely cosmetic changes on otherwise healthy front teeth, more conservative options such as bonding or veneers are usually considered first.

## How it works
The tooth is shaped under local anaesthetic, a scan or impression is taken, and a temporary crown fitted. The permanent crown is cemented at a second visit; some clinics mill same-day crowns.

## Timeline and results
Crowns typically last 10–15 years or more.

## Risks and side effects
Sensitivity after preparation, a small risk that the nerve becomes inflamed and needs root canal treatment, and wear on opposing teeth with some materials.

## Cost factors
Priced per tooth. Material, lab fees and any core build-up required affect the price.
