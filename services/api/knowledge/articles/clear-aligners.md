# Clear aligners

## What it is
Clear aligners are a series of removable, transparent plastic trays, each slightly different, that move teeth gradually. Small tooth-coloured attachments are often bonded to some teeth to help the trays grip.

## Who it suits
Mild to moderate crowding, spacing and some bite issues in adults and teenagers who will wear the trays 20–22 hours a day. Severe rotations, large bite discrepancies, or skeletal jaw problems may need fixed braces or combined orthodontic-surgical care. Gum disease and decay must be treated before any tooth movement.

## How it works
A dentist or orthodontist takes a 3D scan, plans the tooth movement digitally, and provides a new set of trays every 1–2 weeks. Trays are removed for eating and brushing. Check-ups are every 6–10 weeks.

## Timeline and results
Typical treatment runs 6–18 months depending on complexity. **Retainers are required afterwards**, usually worn every night long-term, otherwise teeth tend to drift back.

## Risks and side effects
Pressure or soreness for a day or two with each new tray, a mild lisp at first, and poor results if trays are not worn enough. Root shortening and gum recession are uncommon but possible with any orthodontic treatment.

## Cost factors
Complexity (number of trays and refinements), provider supervision and retainers drive the cost. Budget "direct-to-consumer" aligners with little in-person supervision carry more risk.
