# Composite bonding

## What it is
Bonding uses tooth-coloured composite resin, sculpted directly onto the tooth and hardened with a curing light. It is used to repair small chips, close small gaps, cover stains and reshape uneven edges.

## Who it suits
People with minor chips, small gaps between front teeth, or isolated discoloured areas who want a conservative, usually reversible option. Little or no enamel is removed. Large defects, heavy biting forces or tooth grinding may favour a crown or veneer instead.

## How it works
The surface is lightly roughened, a bonding agent applied, and composite layered and shaped to match neighbouring teeth, then polished. Typically 30–60 minutes per tooth, often in a single visit with no anaesthetic.

## Timeline and results
Bonding usually lasts 3–10 years. It can be repaired or re-polished. Whitening should be done *before* bonding, because composite does not change colour afterwards.

## Risks and side effects
Composite can stain and chip more easily than porcelain. Biting nails, pens or ice shortens its life.

## Cost factors
Priced per tooth. The number of teeth, the complexity of the shaping, and whether whitening is done first all affect the total.
