# Dental implants and bridges

## What it is
An **implant** is a titanium or zirconia post placed in the jawbone to replace a missing tooth root, topped with a crown. A **bridge** replaces a missing tooth with a false tooth anchored to crowns on neighbouring teeth. Removable partial dentures are a lower-cost alternative.

## Who it suits
Implants suit adults with healthy gums and enough jawbone, ideally non-smokers with well-controlled general health (for example diabetes). Bone grafting may be needed first. Bridges suit cases where the neighbouring teeth already need crowns, or when surgery is not wanted.

## How it works
Implant placement is minor surgery under local anaesthetic. The implant integrates with bone over 2–6 months before the final crown is attached. A bridge usually takes two visits over a couple of weeks.

## Timeline and results
Implants can last decades with good care. Bridges commonly last 10–15 years.

## Risks and side effects
Implants carry surgical risks (infection, nerve irritation, sinus involvement for upper back teeth) and can fail to integrate, more often in smokers. Bridges require reshaping healthy neighbouring teeth.

## Cost factors
Implants cost more up front (implant, abutment, crown, possible grafting and imaging). Bridges are priced per unit, counting the false tooth and each supporting crown.
